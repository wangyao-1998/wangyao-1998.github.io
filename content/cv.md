## Research Interests

My research addresses sustainable solid waste management in a changing Earth system. I study the two-way interactions between waste systems and global change and turn the findings into engineering and management solutions, aiming to make waste systems measurable, predictable, and manageable.

**Measuring global impacts of waste facilities.** By cross-checking IPCC-based bottom-up inventories against top-down atmospheric estimates, I revealed systematic underestimation of landfill methane, especially in data-scarce countries and at informal and legacy dumpsites. I combine machine learning, satellite remote sensing, and field data to map waste facilities and build local-to-global inventories toward a global measurement, reporting, and verification (MRV) framework.

**Understanding coupled waste processes under global change.** I identify climate-sensitive controls on waste decay, such as composition, moisture, and temperature (CMT), and translate them into site-specific models, including the CMT method for landfill methane adopted by Climate TRACE. I investigate how global change, such as warming, extreme rainfall, sea-level rise, and shifting waste composition, alters coupled biological, chemical, and physical waste processes, and develop physics-informed process models to predict gas and contaminant fluxes.

**Engineering low-carbon, climate-resilient waste systems.** I combined revised emission estimates and exposure risks to develop low-cost, local mitigation strategies, from food waste reduction and waste-to-energy to landfill gas capture, and co-developed WasteMAP with RMI. I aim to design and field-verify lasting interventions, such as adaptive gas collection, biocovers, and aeration for closed landfills, carbonation of incineration ash, and climate-resilient closure designs.

**Tracing the long-term fate of plastics and microplastics.** I study how land-disposed plastics age, fragment into microplastics, and are released to the environment, drawing on ten-year landfill simulation experiments. I also investigate plastiglomerates, plastic-rock composites that may form in landfills, and how plastics may alter carbon and nitrogen cycling in waste, to inform monitoring, risk assessment, and end-of-life management.

## Research Impact

### Policy and Practical Applications

- **Climate TRACE** adopted my improved landfill methane estimation method to determine site-specific waste decay rates. Its 2025 solid-waste methodology explicitly applies this approach in facility-level emissions estimation.
- **WasteMAP / RMI**: In collaboration with RMI, I contributed to the development of WasteMAP by integrating my improved landfill methane estimation method and mitigation assessment into the platform, and supported data consolidation and landfill identification for city- and facility-level emissions accounting and decision support.
- **OECD**, *Targeting methane emissions to mitigate the risk of climate overshoot* (2025), cited my research to evidence that landfill methane emissions are underestimated and mitigation opportunities are being missed.

### Peer Evaluation

- Örjan Gustafsson (Stockholm University), Jiang Kejun (Energy Research Institute, NDRC, China), and colleagues, in *Ten new insights in climate science 2024*, *One Earth* (2025), recognized my contribution to integrating bottom-up and top-down approaches for methane emissions estimation.
- Daniel J. Jacob (Harvard University) and colleagues, *Environmental Research Letters* (2025), cited my finding that rainfall alters waste decay rates to explain the satellite-observed surge in methane emissions at a US landfill during an abnormally wet year (2020).
- Joannes D. Maasakkers (SRON), Daniel J. Varon (MIT), and colleagues, *Nature* (2025), cited my research to demonstrate that landfill methane model parameters are uncertain and site-specific, supporting the case for independent satellite observations.
- Tianhai Cheng (Chinese Academy of Sciences) and colleagues, *Nature Climate Change* (2025), cited my research to support that meteorological variations drive fluctuations in landfill methane emissions, and that improved landfill management offers a cost-effective mitigation pathway.
- Guanghao Chen (Hong Kong University of Science and Technology) and colleagues, *Nature Cities* (2025), cited my research to establish that landfills generate more than 10% of global anthropogenic methane emissions.
- Beidou Xi (Chinese Research Academy of Environmental Sciences) and colleagues, *Water Research* (2026), cited my research to establish the substantial global climate contribution of landfill methane.
